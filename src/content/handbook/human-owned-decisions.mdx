## Principle

Automation can prepare, compare, and explain. People retain the authority to accept, revise, defer, or stop consequential actions.

## When it matters

Apply this where a recommendation carries uncertainty, changes a durable record, spends resources, or affects another person. The more difficult the reversal, the clearer the review boundary should be.

## Reusable pattern

Represent proposals as bounded options with their evidence, assumptions, and a clear owner. Require an intentional confirmation for the action, then record what was decided and why.

## Failure mode

A system hides uncertainty behind a single recommended action, or turns a suggestion into an irreversible change before the responsible person can inspect it.

## Practical checklist

- State what the system can decide and what it may only recommend.
- Show evidence and uncertainty beside the proposed action.
- Provide accept, revise, defer, and stop paths where appropriate.
- Record the human decision without implying that automation made it.
