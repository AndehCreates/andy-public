## Principle

Important output should trace to a trusted source, durable context, or inspectable system state. A concise answer with visible grounding is more useful than a confident answer that cannot be checked.

## When it matters

Use this principle when a decision depends on prior work, changing evidence, or details that cannot safely live in a single conversation or a person's memory.

## Reusable pattern

Keep the authoritative record close to the work, state what evidence was used, and preserve a handoff when the work pauses. Treat generated summaries as views over that record, not as a replacement authority.

## Failure mode

An assistant or workflow produces a fluent conclusion from stale notes, partial context, or an unverified assumption. The result sounds complete but gives the next reviewer no way to find the missing basis.

## Practical checklist

- Identify the source of record before composing an output.
- Make uncertainty and missing evidence visible.
- Link decisions back to the relevant context or state.
- Preserve a recoverable handoff when work cannot be completed.
