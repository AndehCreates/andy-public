## Principle

Confidence should come from behavior that can be checked: a test, a replayable scenario, an inspection of output, or a clearly recorded limitation.

## When it matters

This matters whenever a system summarizes evidence, coordinates work, ranks options, or makes a claim that could look convincing while being wrong, stale, or incomplete.

## Reusable pattern

Write the expected behavior before implementation, run narrow checks close to the changed boundary, and keep explicit states for blocked, inconclusive, and failed outcomes. Pair automation with a human-readable account of what was checked.

## Failure mode

A demo succeeds on a happy path and is treated as validation. Missing inputs, stale state, recovery, and uncertainty remain untested until a real user encounters them.

## Practical checklist

- Define the behavior and failure condition before building.
- Use the smallest repeatable check that can disprove the claim.
- Test uncertainty, stale state, and recovery paths.
- State what the evidence does not establish.
