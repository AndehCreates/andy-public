## What it solves

Making intentions workable is difficult when desired activity, commitments, and available time are held separately or only in working memory. LifeOS is a local-first workspace that brings them into a workable view while externalizing the changing context around a plan. It is aimed at people who need a calm way to manage activity across real time rather than another generic task list.

## System design

The workspace interface and domain behavior share explicit product contracts. Those contracts connect planning, focus, capture, transition, and re-entry flows to local persistence and the existing synchronization architecture while consequential adjustments remain user-confirmed. Interruption recovery remains a continuity mechanism that helps the plan survive when real life changes it.

## Key decisions and tradeoffs

<DecisionBlock kind="decision" title="Extend the existing state and sync model">
  The design builds on existing state and synchronization surfaces through explicit domain contracts instead of introducing a replacement orchestrator or persistence model.
</DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock kind="tradeoff" title="Make consequential automation confirmable">
  High-consequence or ambiguous automation is represented as a user-confirmed intervention with a safe timeout disposition. This asks for attention at important moments in exchange for preserving user control.
</DecisionBlock>

## Validation

The inspected codebase includes typed application-state and planning contracts, local-first persistence and synchronization surfaces, and a verified type check. Source inspection also confirmed contracts for adjustment proposals, delegated policies, and human interventions.

<DecisionBlock kind="limitation" title="Scenario validation remains open">
  This evidence does not establish production deployment, cross-device reliability, accessibility outcomes, or completed end-to-end user scenarios. Local persistence and synchronization still need continued conflict and durability validation.
</DecisionBlock>

## Human value

LifeOS is intended to lower the cognitive cost of making intentions workable by making commitments, time, context, next actions, and recovery paths visible rather than requiring someone to keep that state in memory.
