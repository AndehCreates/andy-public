# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary visitors are hiring managers, potential collaborators, and technical
peers evaluating Andy's judgment, product craft, and ability to build reliable,
human-centered AI and software systems.

They should be able to understand the positioning quickly, inspect concrete
decisions and evidence within a few minutes, and follow deeper technical paths
without needing access to private source material.

## Product Purpose

The portfolio presents a coherent public body of work around software that
strengthens human capability. It connects projects, systems, principles,
signals, decisions, and validation boundaries so visitors can see both what was
built and how the work was reasoned about.

Success means the public experience demonstrates serious systems thinking and
interface craft through inspectable artifacts rather than inflated titles,
employment claims, or generic capability statements.

## Positioning

The portfolio treats software as infrastructure for human capability. It shows
how grounded context, explicit authority, modular boundaries, evaluation, and
recoverable state become concrete product and engineering decisions.

The system extends human capability without erasing human authority.

## Operating Context

- Repository-owned, reviewed content is the public authority.
- Evidence inventories and source inspection precede public claims.
- Long-form dossiers, diagrams, decision traces, tests, and public-safe source
  links provide the primary proof.
- Visitors move between curated overview surfaces and quieter reading surfaces.
- Core routes and content remain available in static HTML without JavaScript.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Astro 6, strict TypeScript, typed content collections, MDX, and static output.
- Progressive enhancement may improve exploration but cannot own publication,
  navigation, or required meaning.
- Publication state, source availability, and public review are independent.
- Draft and internal material cannot appear in public routes, metadata, feeds,
  filters, relationships, or generated output.
- Public content cannot expose secrets, private URLs or hostnames, filesystem
  paths, personal data, unsupported quantitative claims, or unreviewed media.
- No CMS, database, authentication system, analytics stack, or runtime backend
  is part of the current scope.

## Brand Commitments

- Name the work professionally and precisely.
- Lead with decisions, artifacts, validation, tradeoffs, and human value.
- Avoid inflated labels, generic developer-dashboard language, and repetitive
  claims of "proof."
- Preserve the incumbent dark visual system and its restrained cyan, violet,
  green, and warm-yellow semantic accents.
- Motion explains flow, relationships, or state changes; it does not decorate
  every scroll.

## Evidence on Hand

- Sanitized evidence inventories under `docs/evidence/`.
- Approved project and case-study content under `src/content/`.
- Typed publication, presentation, and relationship contracts under `src/lib/`.
- Unit, content-audit, static-output, accessibility, responsive, no-JavaScript,
  and browser test surfaces under `tests/` and `scripts/`.
- Approved product and connected-catalog specifications under
  `docs/superpowers/specs/`.

Evidence gaps remain explicit. A visual or editorial treatment cannot promote a
pending item or enlarge a claim beyond its reviewed source.

## Product Principles

1. Start with something inspectable: a source, test, interface state, decision
   record, observed failure, or reviewed artifact.
2. Keep consequential human authority understandable and interruptible.
3. Separate responsibilities so runtime, memory, governance, coordination,
   publication, and repository truth do not silently replace one another.
4. State what evidence establishes and what remains outside the validation
   boundary.
5. Make relationships useful by explaining why two artifacts belong together.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Core content and navigation must remain usable without JavaScript. Keyboard
operation, visible focus, semantic landmarks, readable text alternatives,
responsive layouts without horizontal overflow, and reduced-motion behavior are
required across public surfaces.
